# Marketing website — copy

Vendor-facing text for the one-page site. Use this as the source of truth when building UI. Do not invent extra headlines without updating this file.

Tone: clear, calm, specific. Lead with **concrete moments** on the phone, not abstract pain.

## 1. Hero (`#hero`)

**Eyebrow:** Built for WhatsApp sellers buried in chats

**Headline:** Thousands of messages a day. One you. Nothing slips through.

**Subhead:** ChatLogix is your AI shop assistant on WhatsApp. It replies the instant customers write in, turns chats into orders, and keeps you in control — so a flooded inbox stops being the reason your day runs late.

**Scenario (v2):** 47 unread at the market. By evening: 3 new orders, and everyone else already has an answer.

**Primary CTA:** Get started  
**Secondary CTA:** See how it works

**Trust line:** You see every conversation. Pause AI anytime. Reply yourself when it matters.

**Hero visual:** Phone + dashboard mocks always visible (mobile and desktop). Optional HeroCanvas overlay on desktop only. Static SVG fallback when reduced motion.

## 2. The problem (`#problem`)

**Headline:** Your customers already message you on WhatsApp. That's also the problem.

**Intro:** For a small shop, WhatsApp is the store, the counter, and the support desk — until the chats never stop coming.

### Day timeline (v2 — not numbered cards)

| Time | Title | Phone caption |
|---|---|---|
| Morning | You are the whole team | "Do you have this in stock?" — while you're at the market |
| Afternoon | Every chat is a new start | Price, size, delivery — typed again for the third time today |
| Night | Orders hide in the inbox | "Yes, send 2 kg" buried between family chats and voice notes |

**Pull quote (caption style, not giant H2):** "I don't need more customers. I need time to serve the ones I already have."

## 3. The bottleneck (`#bottleneck`)

**Headline:** The bottleneck isn't your products. It's the inbox.

**Intro:** When messages grow from tens to hundreds to thousands, typing every reply yourself is what breaks — not demand.

**Visual (v2):** Before | After toggle driving two phone + dashboard compositions. Optional tiny day-split bar under phones (labels only).

**Phone before (example):** 47 unread, 2 orders packed, assistant off — "You're answering every chat yourself"

**Phone after (example):** 8 unread, 12 orders packed, assistant on — "Assistant clearing routine chats"

**Caption:** Example only — how a busy day can shift from inbox to orders, not live data.

**Closer:** Healthy shops need fast replies and time to complete orders. You should not have to choose.
